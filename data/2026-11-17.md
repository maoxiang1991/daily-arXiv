Abstract (中文): 

Abstract: We present \textbf{DeepBA} (\textbf{Deep} \textbf{B}undle \textbf{A}djustment), a graph neural network (GNN) that recovers camera poses, intrinsics, and scene structure from 2D matches across an arbitrary number of views. Rather than solving a non-linear least-squares problem per scene, DeepBA amortizes bundle adjustment into a learned graph network that iteratively refines its estimate from geometric residuals. Where classical SfM computes its updates analytically and needs careful initialization, DeepBA learns them and converges from a cold start. It is trained with scale-invariant supervision (ground-truth rotation, translation direction, and intrinsics, plus reprojection self-consistency), so datasets with arbitrary metric units can be mixed without per-scene normalization. DeepBA is decoupled from the matcher. At 2.04M parameters, it outperforms prior learned SfM from matches on scene reconstruction and on most pose metrics, is competitive with classical SfM at moderate accuracy thresholds and overtakes it in wall time as the view count grows, and approaches monolithic image-to-geometry transformers such as VGGT ($\sim$1.2B parameters) with greater modularity. Abstract: Long-horizon navigation from indoors to outdoors remains an open problem for humanoid robots: one instruction covers rooms, doors and roads, and the camera moves with the gait. Worken, AGIBOT's measure of productive robot work, depends on robots that reach their workplaces autonomously. We present X-Nav, to our knowledge the first system to take a bipedal humanoid through a language-instructed long-horizon mission from indoors to outdoors. X-Nav covers a pipeline from data to deployment. Its data engine collects navigation data in open-source scenes, in digital twins of deployment sites and on real robots. Its navigation model, MELIOR, takes only RGB images from the robot's built-in camera and comes in two versions: MELIOR-A predicts actions, and MELIOR-N predicts a navigation goal. Its agentic workflow, X-Navarch, turns a brief human instruction into sub-tasks and deploys MELIOR on the robot with explicit target navigation and obstacle avoidance. On the Lingxi X2, X-Nav completes a mission of about a hundred metres from an office lobby to a store in a neighbouring building; LiDAR and odometry are used for target approach and low-level control. The mission is completed with both versions of MELIOR: the robot follows a change of destination and walks through a door from the lobby to the road, and with X-Navarch it also passes static and dynamic obstacles on the route. During deployment, MELIOR is adapted with data collected in a digital twin of the site; the adaptation raises its closed-loop success rate in the twin, and the adapted model is then deployed on the robot at the real site. In addition, MELIOR achieves competitive results on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks.

Abstract: Multi-view visual augmentation often seeks consistency across cameras, but it is unclear which forms of consistency matter for downstream control. We use simulation-state replay to manipulate cross-view coupling while preserving the per-camera perturbation distributions, physical trajectories, and action labels. Across seven LIBERO manipulation tasks, marginal-matched changes in global appearance, ghost injection, and target color have small average costs for a late-fusion Diffusion Policy: point estimates are near zero or negative, and the largest upper endpoint of the 90% posterior credible intervals is 2.55 percentage points. In contrast, geometrically anti-coupled observations reduce success by 3.30 percentage points relative to coupled observations (90% CrI [0.64, 6.28]), with displacement magnitude, direction marginals, and the number of affected views matched. Camera interventions reveal substantial dependence on wrist-view geometry, while feature analysis shows appearance sensitivity patterns associated with the factors randomized during training. ACT shows a higher target-color cost estimate than DP, with uncertainty that includes zero and sensitivity to task composition. Within the tested tasks and policy configurations, these findings motivate selective consistency constraints: appearance coupling can sometimes be relaxed, whereas task-relevant geometry and cross-view spatial correspondence warrant protection.

Abstract: Optimizing robot poses and the map simultaneously has been shown to provide more accurate SLAM results. However, for non-feature based SLAM approaches, directly optimizing all the robot poses and the whole map will greatly increase the computational cost, making SLAM problems difficult to solve in large-scale environments. To solve the 2D non-feature based SLAM problem in large-scale environments more accurately and efficiently, we propose the grid-based submap joining method. Specifically, we first formulate the 2D grid-based submap joining problem as a non-linear least squares (NLLS) form to optimize the global occupancy map and local submap frames simultaneously. We then prove that in solving the NLLS problem using Gauss-Newton (GN) method, the increments of the poses in each iteration are independent of the occupancy values of the global occupancy map. Based on this property, we propose a poseonly GN algorithm equivalent to full GN method to solve the NLLS problem. The proposed submap joining algorithm is very efficient due to the independent property and the pose-only solution. Evaluations using simulations and publicly available practical 2D laser datasets confirm the outperformance of our proposed method compared to the state-of-the-art methods in terms of efficiency and accuracy, as well as the ability to solve the grid-based SLAM problem in very large-scale environments.

Abstract: 2D-to-3D image registration is a key component for several medical imaging purposes such as intraoperative guidance, enabling the alignment between preoperative 3D volumes and real-time 2D images. While conventional optimisation-based methods often struggle with non-convex objectives, sensitivity to initialisation, and high computational demands, deep learning (DL) has emerged as a promising alternative, offering improved alignment robustness and the potential for real-time performance. This review provides a comprehensive analysis of DL frameworks developed to address the challenges inherent in 2D-to-3D registration. We first outline how core concepts of DL are adapted to the registration problem. We then examine recent methodological advancements from multiple perspectives, including pipeline design, architectural innovations, and domain adaptation strategies. Particular attention is given to quantitative metrics, highlighting how performance is defined and measured across studies. Across the 41 included studies, we find that direct regression is the most common pipeline (23 studies, against 18 iterative ones), that hybrid CNN-attention architectures are gaining ground, and that research remains heavily concentrated on X-ray-to-CT registration. We conclude by identifying the absence of per-case uncertainty estimation and the strong neglect of temporal or sequential modelling as the two methodological gaps most consequential for clinical readiness, and discuss state-space models as a way to add temporal memory within intraoperative latency budgets. Further opportunities lie in more principled domain adaptation and in the integration of foundation models for representation learning.
