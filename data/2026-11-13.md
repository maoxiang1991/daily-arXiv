Abstract: Multi-camera rigs ship pre-calibrated from the manufacturer, but their calibration goes stale each time the rig is repositioned, and drifts continuously through wear, thermal cycling, and vibration. Recovering it on-the-fly is hard: classical bundle adjustment needs a fresh multi-view capture and a global optimization whose cost grows with the rig, ruling out real-time use. In this paper we present RECALL, a feed-forward method that recalibrates a movable multi-camera rig in a single pass. From the nominal OEM calibration as an anchor and a brief observation of a known object, it predicts each camera's current intrinsics and extrinsics, written as a shared global rotation of the rig composed with a small per-camera drift. Instead of regressing the calibration in one shot, RECALL refines its estimate with a learned, rig-coupled recurrent operator that reprojects through a differentiable projection and applies attention across the cameras at each step, and solves for the shared rotational gauge in-network with a learned Gauss-Newton step rather than regressing it.
